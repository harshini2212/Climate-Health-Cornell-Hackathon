## 07:00 — rung 1, pooled NUTS (model/hazard.py, model/fit.py, model/score.py)
900,000 veteran-days (10k veterans × the 90 days before the eval holdout) → **106,661 binomial cells, 8.4×**, grouped on the exact active design row so the likelihood is *identical* to the Bernoulli panel rather than approximate (asserted in `test_hazard_toy.py`; the old "50× fewer rows" in the README was a guess and is now the measured 8.4×). 4 chains × 500+500, diagonal metric, float64: **r-hat max 1.0082, 0 divergences, ESS min 1,390 bulk / 1,069 tail**, 64 leapfrog steps an iteration with 0% tree-depth saturation — clears SPEC 6.5's r-hat < 1.05 and the stricter Vehtari et al. (2021) bar too. The toy acceptance fit (200 × 30) is ~10 s with zero divergences. `make fit` takes 37 minutes here (2,199 s, honestly timed); SPEC's 4×1000/1000 takes ~40, so it is cut to 500/500 under SPEC 6.2's own fallback.

**Two things the other lanes need.** (1) **The last 30 days are not fitted**, so `eval/calibration.py`'s window is genuinely out of sample; `fit.HOLDOUT_DAYS` is pinned to `calibration.HOLDOUT_DAYS` by a test. (2) **Predict with the terms you fitted.** Rung 1's main effects are marginal over the 16 interactions it has no column for — `alpha (heat)` comes back near −4.4 against a generating −6.0 because nearly every August day is hot and it absorbs them. `posterior.nc` carries all 35 terms (interactions at their priors, as `recovery.py` requires) but `score.py` zeroes the carried ones: adding them back on top of main effects that already contain them would count them twice and score a hot day ~4× too high. For the demo that means at rung 1 the medication and SiteDown *interaction* chips go quiet and fitted `psi_sitedown` (1.45 against a true 1.5) carries the closure story instead. A clean clone has no `posterior.nc`, so `make demo` is still rung 0 with every chip — switching the board to rung 1 is Rahul's call, not a side effect.

Two traps worth knowing. `az.rhat(idata).to_array()` broadcasts every variable's dims against every other's, so 35 terms with auto-named dims is a 5³⁵-cell array and `eval/recovery.diagnostics()` dies; `fit.py` gives every term the shared `need` dim, which keeps it at 2,100. And JAX dispatches asynchronously, so a clock around `mcmc.run()` measures *enqueue*: `runtime_s` read 11.3 s for a 37-minute fit until `run()` started blocking on the draws.
